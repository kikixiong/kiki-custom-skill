# Introduction Skill

## Function

The Introduction expands the Abstract's assertions into a persuasive chain. It
must make the gap unavoidable, show why the method follows from the insight,
and preview evidence without reproducing Method or Experiments.

## Adaptive paragraph roles

A common map is:

1. task, current practice, and concrete tension;
2. conceptual and practical difficulty or exact gap;
3. observation/evidence figure;
4. inference or early indicator that motivates the method;
5. method overview and why it may work;
6. evidence summary;
7. contributions.

Merge, split, or reorder based on the paper. The successful six-paragraph
project structure is an example, not a universal template.

## Build from an idea

1. Choose an opening altitude: task when readers need context, failure when the
   phenomenon is distinctive, or system constraint when it defines the gap.
2. State a reasonable existing response before criticizing it.
3. Define the missing criterion precisely.
4. Separate co-equal difficulties with parallel structure; do not confuse them
   with a temporal chain.
5. Introduce evidence at the point where it creates a new premise.
6. Derive the insight from the evidence at its licensed strength.
7. Present method actions as direct responses to the diagnosed gap.
8. Preview why the design could work.
9. Summarize experiments by claims, not table order.
10. Write contributions as distinct scientific additions.

## Figure 1 as narrative evidence

For each panel record:

| Panel | Question | Setup | Observation | Licensed inference | Used by |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Narrate panels in inferential order, which should usually match visual reading
order. State what is measured before the conclusion. Do not make a correlation
figure prove causality, and do not introduce the conclusion before the figure
that supports it.

## Paragraph test

For every paragraph ask:

- Why must this paragraph exist?
- What does it add beyond the previous paragraph?
- What prerequisite does it create?
- What breaks if it is removed?
- Is its final sentence a conclusion/handoff or a redundant restatement?

Two consecutive paragraphs that both say existing methods fail probably need
distinct roles, stronger differentiation, or merging.

## Method preview

Include:

- method identity;
- major actions or component roles;
- their dependency;
- why they answer the gap.

Exclude:

- equations;
- low-level update rules;
- implementation-only hyperparameters;
- a component list with no rationale.

## Results preview

State the main evidence pattern and scope. Dense numbers are usually deferred,
but a decisive number is allowed when it establishes effect size or credibility.
Do not enforce a universal “no numbers” rule.

## Contributions

Each item should contain:

- contribution object;
- scientific or technical action;
- why it matters;
- evidence class when appropriate.

Avoid mechanically repeating the method paragraph. Contributions may cover
problem/observation, method, and empirical evidence, but should reflect the
paper's actual novelty.

## Transition engineering

Use conceptual handoff: the next paragraph should consume a premise produced by
the previous one. Lexical overlap can help readers but is not a hard equality
rule.

## Introduction anti-patterns

- generic field-importance opening;
- Related Work list in the gap paragraph;
- two paragraphs repeating the same limitation;
- observation presented as self-evident;
- figure cited parenthetically after the conclusion;
- method details before insight;
- contributions that introduce new claims.
