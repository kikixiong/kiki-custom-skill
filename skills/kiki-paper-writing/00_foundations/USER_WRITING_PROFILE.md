# User Writing Profile

Use this as a decision policy, not a style adjective list.

## Preference hierarchy

### 1. Logic and accuracy before elegance

- **Behavior:** retain the version with the clearer mechanism, referent, scope,
  and evidence boundary even when another is smoother.
- **Reject:** a polished sentence whose causal link is only implied.
- **Evidence:** Explicit and repeatedly inferred; High confidence.

### 2. Concrete mechanism before abstract label

- State the failure, operation, or observed behavior before naming it as a
  general limitation or insight.
- Abstract nouns are acceptable after their referent is operationally clear.
- **Evidence:** Strongly inferred across Abstract and Introduction; High.

### 3. Explain fully, then stop

- Include the missing premise or mechanism that makes the logic complete.
- Delete a final clause that merely renames or praises what was just explained.
- Concision means no lost reasoning, not minimum word count.
- **Evidence:** Explicit/repeated; High.

### 4. One paragraph, one main narrative job

- A paragraph may contain setup, evidence, and transition, but only one primary
  conclusion.
- Split a paragraph that simultaneously establishes the gap, introduces the
  method, and reports results.
- **Evidence:** Explicit/repeated; High.

### 5. Every sentence must add information or inference

- A sentence should introduce a premise, mechanism, evidence, implication, or
  necessary transition.
- Remove “paper-like” sentences that only say the topic is important.
- **Evidence:** Strongly inferred; High.

### 6. Evidence before finding strength

- Anchor observations in the relevant analysis, figure, experiment, or theorem.
- Use `indicates` or `is associated with` when causality is not established.
- **Evidence:** Explicit/repeated; High.

### 7. Preserve all real contributions without listing machinery

- Abstract and Introduction must cover all major contribution classes.
- Compress modules into method actions and rationale; do not make a component
  inventory the story.
- **Evidence:** Explicit/repeated; High.

### 8. Figures are part of the argument

- A motivation figure should create the observation or inference that licenses
  the method, not appear as decoration after the claim.
- A framework figure must share names, symbols, stages, and data flow with
  Method Overview.
- **Evidence:** Explicit/repeated; High.

### 9. Method prose should expose rationale and execution

- Prefer connection → formula → immediate explanation → operational behavior.
- Keep mechanism-defining details; move only implementation-only settings.
- **Evidence:** Explicit/repeated; High.

### 10. Minimal, stable notation

- One symbol has one semantic role.
- Define symbols just in time and avoid visual collisions.
- Distinguish model, distribution, mapping, condition, state, and prediction.
- **Evidence:** Explicit/repeated; High.

### 11. Natural direct voice

- Prefer specific verbs and ordinary transitions over stock phrases such as
  `To alleviate this limitation` or `It is worth noting that`.
- Do not replace every occurrence mechanically; judge function and rhythm.
- **Evidence:** Explicit/repeated; High.

### 12. Parallel structure for genuine categories

- When multiple difficulties are co-equal categories, use parallel grammar and
  comparable abstraction levels.
- Do not force parallelism onto a causal sequence.
- **Evidence:** Explicit; High.

### 13. Section position governs detail

- Abstract states conclusions; Introduction explains the problem and insight;
  Method formalizes; Experiments validate.
- A technically true detail can still be wrong for its narrative location.
- **Evidence:** Repeated; High.

### 14. Bounded claims beat impressive claims

- Prefer a narrower defensible claim over universal language.
- Avoid `guarantees`, `eliminates`, `perfectly`, or causal verbs without the
  required proof or intervention.
- **Evidence:** Explicit/repeated; High.

## Conditional preferences, not universal bans

- Introduction usually summarizes headline results without dense numbers, but
  a decisive number is allowed when it establishes scale or the venue expects
  it.
- Abstract usually avoids benchmark inventories, yet one result or model name
  is allowed when central to identity or evidence.
- A six-paragraph Introduction is one successful project pattern, not a law.
- Fixed word counts are venue budgets, not quality criteria.
- Method prose should be compact, but formulas do not automatically carry more
  information than clear language.

## Decision test

When two versions are both grammatical, ask in order:

1. Which is scientifically accurate?
2. Which makes the causal/inferential edge explicit?
3. Which performs the correct local role?
4. Which covers the required mechanism or contribution?
5. Which is globally consistent?
6. Only then: which is shorter and more natural?
