# Workflow: Global Assembly and Final Audit

## Assemble by canonical source

1. List the canonical file for every component.
2. Separate confirmed from synchronized state.
3. Assemble without editing source components in place.
4. Record any glue sentence or formatting change.

## Semantic audit

Build a cross-section matrix for:

- task, gap, insight, and method identity;
- contribution count and ordering;
- claim proposition, strength, and scope;
- method/component names;
- metrics and result directions;
- symbols, branch roles, state, and execution order;
- figure/table interpretation.

Check that every Introduction promise has evidence and every headline
experiment answers a previously motivated question.

## Mechanical audit

- compile from a clean enough state to expose missing dependencies;
- inspect warnings, undefined labels/citations, duplicate labels, and BibTeX;
- render and visually inspect all pages;
- inspect embedded figure text, legends, arrows, symbols, and resolution;
- check floats, whitespace, font size, clipping, and table readability;
- verify title/author/anonymous-submission settings.

## Reviewer attack pass

Ask:

- Is the gap a straw man?
- Is the insight only correlation?
- Does the method need every component?
- Are comparisons fair and scope-matched?
- Are negative cases or limitations hidden?
- Could the claimed improvement come from more compute/data/parameters?
- Is reproducibility sufficient?
- Does any caption or table invite a stronger claim than the text supports?

## Triage

1. Fatal: incorrect science, broken references/compilation, unreadable assets.
2. Logic: missing warrant, overclaim, inconsistent method or experiment.
3. Style: ambiguity, redundancy, generic phrasing.
4. Polish: rhythm and optional wording.

Deliver the highest-risk issues first.
